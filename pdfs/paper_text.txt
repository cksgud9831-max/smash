Abstract?봎odern warfare is rapidly evolving towards 
unmanned and intelligent trends. Currently, one of the 
technical difficulties in unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) 
airborne fire control systems is how to accurately and quickly 
solve shooting data. Therefore, in this paper, we propose a 
method for onboard fire control calculations based on variable 
angle integration. First, the ballistic model equations are 
mathematically transformed to transform the time integration 
into an angular integration. In each iteration, the target is 
always treated as a moving state, and the relation between the 
hit-point distance and the trajectory inclination angle is 
introduced. The firing elements were modified by Newton 
descending method. The variable angle integral method 
reduces the calculation times of the ballistic differential 
equation and improves the accuracy of the end of the hit point 
under the same operation conditions. The results of the 
simulation experiments show that the proposed method is 
computationally accurate and time-efficient compared to 
conventional methods, providing some reference for the study 
of airborne fire control midpoint and firing element 
calculations.

I. INTRODUCTION 
With the development of network and information 
technology, unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) combat has 
become one of the crucial tasks in modern warfare, making it 
easier to carry firepower weapons for effective strikes. 
Among them, the accuracy and speed at which the various 
elements are solved are the most critical factors affecting the 
accuracy of airborne fire control. Therefore, it is important to 
design a fast and accurate algorithm for fire control solution. 
Reference [1] uses interpolation algorithm to solve 
uncontrolled trajectory in three-dimensional space. Although 
it meets real-time requirements, it requires a large amount of 
memory space and has limited accuracy. References [2-4]
are based on CE-LSTM, Bi LSTM, and  BP neural networ k 
algorithms for predicting external ballistic impact points, 
which improves the accuracy and speed of prediction. 
However, their trained models are only applicable to a single 
type of projectile and environmental conditions, leading to
limited generality in practical applications. In this regard,

The fourth-order Runge-Kutta method is a single-step 
numerical solution that uses only the information from the 
previous step in the calculation and requires the computation 
of four function values at each step; Whereas Adams linear 
multi-step prediction only requires the computation of two

which reduces the computational effort and improves the 
accuracy. In this paper, we use the idea of converting the 
four-order Runge-Kutta method to the four-order Adams 
predictive correction method to improve the accuracy, and 
the idea of changing the step size to shorten the solution 
speed. The detailed derivation process of universal variable 
step Adams is given in reference [11].
D. Step size selection 
Since UAV-mounted fire control normally strikes ground 
targets, if the angle a1 between the target and the launch point 
is known, a complete ballistic curve can be obtained by 
integrating angle a on [0,a1], and limiting the unknown 
integral of the traditional time integral to the known limit of 
the Angle integral. 
Compared to the time integration method, the angle 
integration method increases the integration step size and has 
a lower error divergence, so that the computation can be 
completed in a smaller number of steps and the 
computational speed is increased while the accuracy is 
maintained. 
E. Prediction correction 
In a real-world scenario, if the prediction correction is not 
applied, there will be a large error distance between the 
predicted impact point and the actual impact point, and the 
subsequent impact accuracy will be improved after the 
prediction correction. Since the lift-drag coefficient in the 
ballistic equation is the main effect factor of the errors, and 
m o s t  o f  t h e m  a r e  o b t a i n e d  f r o m  e m p i r i c a l  f o r m u l a e  o r  
simulations, they deviate from the actual environment. In this 
way, the coincidence drag lift coefficient is set. The 
coincidence drag lift coefficient is corrected after the 
elements of the first test projectile landing site are obtained. 
The elements of the predicted landing site and the 
information of the test projectile landing site are corrected 
within a certain error by an iterative correction method.
